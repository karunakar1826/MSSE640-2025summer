\# Easy problems that are hard for LLMs.



One way to get a better understanding of what LLMs can and cannot do is to look at some of the seemingly simple questions that trip them up. Find some examples by experimenting or searching online. Discuss why these problems are hard for LLMs even after several years of intensive effort to improve their performance — and what that tells us about their capacities now and in the future.



##### **Response:**



LLMs often struggle with tasks that seem simple to humans. One example is basic arithmetic like “17 + 28.” While this seems trivial, LLMs sometimes get it wrong because they predict tokens based on patterns rather than truly calculating. This reveals their lack of inherent mathematical reasoning.



Another case is logical reasoning. Given a statement like “Alice is taller than Bob, Bob is taller than Carol. Who is the shortest?”, LLMs can fail to keep track of relationships. They aren't performing logic in a structured way — they're generating text based on probability, not deductive rules.



Commonsense questions are another challenge. For example, asking “Can you wear shoes on your hands?” may result in strange or incorrect answers. That’s because commonsense knowledge is often implicit and not stated directly in text data, making it hard for LLMs to learn.



Temporal reasoning is also difficult. A question like “If I was born in 1990, how old will I be in 2025?” can trip up models because they don’t have a dynamic understanding of time. They rely on training data and don’t really “know” what year it is.



Ambiguous statements also pose a problem. In “The chicken didn’t cross the road because it was too tired,” LLMs may misinterpret who “it” refers to. Humans use world knowledge and context to resolve ambiguity, something LLMs don’t fully grasp.



These challenges show that LLMs still lack true reasoning, world understanding, and context awareness — they’re powerful text predictors, not thinkers. Continued development will need to combine language skills with logic, grounding, and better memory.
