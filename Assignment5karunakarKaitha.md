#### **When a Deepfake Spreads Misinformation: Who Is Responsible?**



##### **1. The Model (the AI system itself):**



The AI model is a tool without consciousness or intent. It generates outputs based on training data and algorithms.



Models themselves do not bear moral or legal responsibility since they lack agency. However, the design and training influence the outputs.



##### **2. The Developer (creators of the model):**



Developers/designers build and train the models. They decide what data to use, how the model works, and what safeguards to implement.



They can be held responsible if they knowingly create tools that facilitate deception or fail to incorporate safety measures to prevent misuse.



Accountability may also hinge on transparency: Have developers disclosed the limitations and risks of their models? Did they implement detection or watermarking techniques?



##### **3. The User (person who uses the model to create and spread content):**



Users have agency and intent. If they intentionally create and distribute deepfakes to deceive or harm, they are clearly responsible.



Users exploit the technology, and responsibility rests heavily on their ethical and legal behavior.



##### **4. Platform and Society:**



Platforms that host or distribute deepfakes may share responsibility for moderating harmful content.



Broader societal frameworks (laws, norms) shape how accountability is enforced and what liabilities exist.



#### **Accountability in Software Engineering for Bugs that Escape Testing**



##### **1. The Developer:**



Developers are responsible for writing and reviewing code, unit tests, and adhering to best practices.



If bugs escape testing due to negligence or poor practices, accountability lies with the development team or individuals.



##### **2. The QA/Test Team:**

##### 

Responsible for designing comprehensive test cases and catching bugs before release.



Inadequate testing or missed test cases can place some responsibility on QA.



##### **3. Management and Process:**



If the software development lifecycle (SDLC) is rushed or lacks proper quality gates, management bears responsibility for creating conditions that lead to bugs escaping.



Lack of resources, unrealistic deadlines, or poor communication contribute to accountability.



##### **4. The Nature of Software:**



Some bugs are inevitable due to complexity; absolute zero-defect software is unrealistic. Responsibility includes accepting that some bugs will escape, but minimizing their impact and fixing them promptly.



#### **Summary: Shared Responsibility and Context**



In generative AI misuse, responsibility is shared but most ethically on the user for misuse and on developers for enabling safeguards.



In SE, responsibility is distributed across developers, testers, and managers, with shared accountability for quality
